## Reflection

For Assignment 2, I changed how I directed the agent based on feedback from Assignment 1. Previously, the site was functional, but the interaction was conceptually simple and I relied too much on the agent as a content generator.

For **SLOP3030 — Dashboard Language: How Cars Talk to Drivers**, I used a more deliberate process. Instead of asking the agent to generate all twelve weeks at once, I chose a depth-first approach and used Week 3 as a flagship example. I asked the agent to first establish a reusable standard for weekly content, visual design, lecture slides, exercises, case studies, and interaction quality.

A major improvement was requiring every interaction to explain genuine complexity. The Warning Priority Lab, for example, is not a simple warning-light quiz. Students must prioritise simultaneous alerts using urgency, visibility, meaning, modality, timing, and required driver action. This makes the interaction reveal trade-offs rather than just display information.

I also required each weekly lecture deck to contain at least twenty substantive slides, including real vehicle examples, diagrams, process flows, comparisons, exercises, and worked solutions. This helped avoid shallow or repetitive AI-generated content.

The visual system was also refined beyond the default template. Rather than copying a literal car dashboard, the site borrows automotive information-design principles such as clear hierarchy, restrained alert colours, structured panels, and consistent status states.

The biggest improvement was learning to direct the agent through problem identification, alternatives, decisions, revision, and validation, instead of simply requesting finished output.
