## Before the studio

Find, or photograph, the instrument cluster of at least three cars from
different categories — for example a performance car, a family car, and a
commercial or fleet vehicle. Bring images or just the make and model so the
group can look them up together; a photographed owner's-manual page works as
well as a photo of the dashboard itself.

## In the studio

Working in pairs, lay the three clusters side by side and answer the same
question of each one: which instrument is centred and largest, and what does
that position claim about what its driver needs fastest? Pairs then swap
findings across the room and argue two specific cases: does the layout match
how the car is actually likely to be driven, and would a first-time driver of
that exact car be able to read its "normal" zone without ever having driven
it before — or only its printed danger zone?

## Afterwards

Write the comparison up as an argument, not a description: pick two of the
three clusters and defend which layout serves its driver better, and why.
This feeds directly into next week, where the same question gets asked of a
dashboard that has no zones left to read at all — only a light.
