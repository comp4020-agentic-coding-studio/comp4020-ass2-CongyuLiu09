## Before the studio

Pick one dashboard alert before you arrive — a seatbelt chime, a speed-limit
warning, a tyre-pressure telltale, a lane-departure alert, anything covered
so far this course. Do five minutes of research into how it's mandated,
rated, or implemented in two different countries or regions, and bring
whatever you find, even if it's incomplete.

## In the studio

Working in pairs, students trade findings and pressure-test each other's
comparison: is the difference actually regulatory (a mandate exists in one
market and not the other), commercial (a manufacturer chose standard versus
optional), or just a naming/localisation difference dressed up as a bigger
gap than it is? Each pair then prepares a two-minute case to the room arguing
which market's version of their alert better serves the driver, and has to
name, honestly, what the other market's choice is optimising for instead —
cost, a different accident profile, a different regulatory priority, or
simple inertia. Idris pushes back on any pair that treats a market's choice
as simply wrong rather than as a different set of trade-offs.

## Afterwards

Write up your alert comparison in a few sentences and keep it — it's a
compact, concrete example worth having on hand if your final project touches
on regulation, localisation, or the assumption that a dashboard's vocabulary
is universal.
