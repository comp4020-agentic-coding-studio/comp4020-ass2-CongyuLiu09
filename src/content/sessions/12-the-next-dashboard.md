## Before the studio

Bring a near-final draft of your alert vocabulary from *The Instrument
Cluster You'd Trust* — the full list of alerts with their triggers, channels
and urgency hierarchy, even if the written defence isn't finished. A rough
table or diagram is fine; a polished document is not the point of today.

## In the studio

Idris pairs students by vehicle type where possible, so each partner brings a
genuinely different context to judge against. Each presenter gets five
minutes to walk their vocabulary through as a whole system — not alert by
alert, but the relationships between them: what shares a channel, what
escalates into what, what happens if two alerts fire at once. The partner's
job is to find one place the system doesn't hold together, and to say so
specifically, drawing on any week's material that applies — an unclear
red/amber boundary, a channel that would fail in the stated context, an
alert whose false-alarm cost hasn't been considered. The room then rotates.

## Afterwards

Write down the one change your crit partner's feedback is prompting before
you leave — not a list of everything raised, the single change most worth
making with the days remaining before Wednesday's submission.
