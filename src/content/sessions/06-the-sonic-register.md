## Before the studio

No preparation required. Come as you are; the exercise depends on hearing
these sounds without context, so don't look up dashboard alert clips
beforehand.

## In the studio

Idris plays a set of real dashboard alert sounds back to back, stripped of
any visual context — a seatbelt chime, a low-fuel ding, a lane-departure
warning, and park-assist proximity beeping among them, unlabelled and in a
shuffled order. Working alone first, everyone writes down a guessed
ranking from "can wait" to "respond now," plus one sentence per sound
describing what about it produced that ranking. Only after rankings are
locked in does the group compare notes and the sounds are named. The
interesting disagreements aren't about which sound is loudest — they're
about which sounds two people ranked in opposite orders, and why one
person heard "insistent" where another heard merely "repetitive."

## Afterwards

Pick one alert from the set that your ranking got wrong relative to its
real-world stakes, and write down what specifically misled you: was it
too calm for what it signals, or too frantic? Keep that note — Idris
returns to it when we look at alarm fatigue and false alarms later in the
course.
