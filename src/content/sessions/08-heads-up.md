## Before the studio

Bring your Redesign a Warning draft (due this Friday) in whatever state it's
in — even a chosen alert with no rationale written yet counts.

## In the studio

Idris hands out a printed driver's-eye photograph of a windscreen with no HUD
graphics on it. In pairs, students sketch directly onto the printout where a
lane-departure warning should appear, then do the same on a fresh copy for a
following-distance warning, using tracing paper so both can be compared
against the same base photo. The placements are then pinned up together and
argued over: who put the warning low and small, who put it large and central,
and what each pair was optimising for — speed of noticing versus how much of
the actual road the graphic covers. The session closes by testing every
placement against one hard case: a driver a head shorter than the one the
photo was taken from, whose eye position no longer lines up with anyone's
sketch.

## Afterwards

Keep both annotated sketches — they're useful evidence for the "craft and
presentation" criterion on Assessment 2 if your redesigned alert involves a
HUD or windscreen element. Idris is available for the rest of the session to
workshop Redesign a Warning drafts ahead of Friday's deadline.
