## Before the studio

Photograph or sketch one telltale from a real dashboard — yours, a family
member's, a manual, or a press photo if nothing else is available. Find out,
even roughly, what triggers it: a pressure switch, a voltage comparator, a
diagnostic trouble code. You don't need the exact number, just enough to know
it's a threshold on something continuous.

## In the studio

Working in pairs, each person presents their telltale and its trigger, then
the pair sorts both examples along a line from "genuinely binary" (a door
ajar, a seatbelt unbuckled — there is no meaningful middle state) to
"continuous value flattened" (oil pressure, coolant temperature, tyre
pressure). For anything on the flattened end, argue where you'd put a second
threshold if you were allowed one, and what that buys the driver. Idris
collects one example per pair on the board and the studio ends by voting on
the single worst offender in the room.

## Afterwards

Write up your telltale's classification and your proposed second threshold
(or your argument that none is needed) in two or three sentences. Keep it —
week 4 asks you to give that same telltale a symbol, and week 10 comes back
to ask whether adding states made it more trustworthy or just noisier.
