## Before the studio

Find one dashboard you have real access to — your own car, a family member's,
a rental, or a manual/press photo if none of those exist for you. Pick a
single element on it: one gauge, one telltale light, one icon, one warning
chime you remember. Bring a photo, sketch, or written description. Don't
overthink the choice — an ordinary fuel gauge is as useful here as anything
exotic.

## In the studio

In small groups, each person presents their element in under two minutes:
what it looks like, what it's telling the driver, and what channel it's
using to do it. The group then tries to answer, for each: could this have
been designed differently — a light instead of a needle, a chime instead of
a light — and what would that change about how fast a driver understands it
and how much attention it costs them. Idris runs this as a fast round-robin
rather than a discussion, so keep your first pass brief; the depth comes
from comparing across the group's different cars.

## Afterwards

Write two or three sentences classifying your element in the terms the group
used, and note one thing another student's example made you notice about
your own. Keep this — later weeks build directly on the vocabulary from this
first pass.
