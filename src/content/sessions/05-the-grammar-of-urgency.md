## Before the studio

Bring a clear photo of an instrument cluster with at least three telltales lit
at once — your own car, a manual, or a manufacturer press shot is fine, as
long as you can see genuine red, amber, and green or blue in the same frame.
A cluttered warning-lights reference sheet from an owner's manual works well
too.

## In the studio

Idris supplies a colour-blindness simulation tool (deuteranopia, protanopia
and tritanopia filters) running on a shared screen. Each student runs their
photo through all three filters and records what happens to their telltales:
which pairs that were clearly different colours become the same washed-out
shade, and whether the icon shape or position rescues the distinction once
colour can't. Work in pairs — one person nominates a telltale, the other
predicts what will happen to it under each filter before you actually run it.

## Afterwards

Write up the one pair that broke worst under simulation and the one telltale
on your cluster that survived every filter intact — then say, in a sentence
each, what design choice made the survivor survive. This becomes your worked
example for the mini knowledge check in the week 5 deck if you're asked to
present it.
