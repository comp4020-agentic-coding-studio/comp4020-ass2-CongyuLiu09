# Policies and support

## Late work and extensions

Work submitted after the deadline loses 5% of the available mark per day,
capped at five days, after which it is marked as not submitted. If something
outside your control is going to make you late — illness, a genuine
emergency, equipment failure you can document — email the convenor before the
deadline, not after it. An extension asked for in advance is a formality; one
asked for afterwards is a much harder conversation.

## Academic integrity and AI assistance

You may use AI tools to help you draft, structure, or check your work. You
may not submit AI output as your own analysis without having done the
analysis yourself first — a marker can generally tell the difference between
"a redesign argued from having actually looked at the dashboard" and "a
redesign argued from having asked a model what dashboards are usually wrong
with," and only one of those meets the brief. Declare significant AI
assistance briefly at the top of your submission: which tool, for what part
of the work. Undeclared assistance that materially shaped your analysis is
treated as a breach of academic integrity, not as a shortcut.

## Accessibility

If you have a disability, ongoing condition, or other circumstance that
affects how you can participate in studios or complete assessments, contact
the convenor early in the semester — adjustments arranged in week 2 are far
easier to make well than the same adjustments arranged in week 11. This
applies to the format of submitted work as much as to attendance; if a
brief's default format doesn't work for you, ask what else would.

## Getting help

Studio time is for questions about the week's material and work in progress;
email is for anything that needs a considered written answer or touches your
mark. See the [people page](/people/) for who to ask and when.
